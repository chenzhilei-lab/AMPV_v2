\begin{abstract}
Aggregate benchmark scores can conceal where an astronomical measurement workflow becomes unreliable as observing conditions change. AMPV is a reusable experimental-design template for retaining that conditional structure: matched stochastic identities are propagated across explicit stress coordinates, workflow information classes are kept distinct, and continuous error and operational vulnerability are reported cell by cell rather than collapsed into a global ranking. The primary comet-like experiment evaluates workflows A--E on five reference-contrast levels and three contamination levels, using 300 matched identities to form 4,500 condition-specific scenes and 22,500 workflow evaluations. A separately frozen compact-PSF extension uses 300 identities and 18,000 evaluations of blind workflows A, B, C, and E. In the comet-like family, all 60 adjacent-reference-contrast paired comparisons had 95\% bootstrap intervals below zero for the change in absolute error, whereas contamination effects were not uniform. In contaminated compact-PSF cells, all 32 adjacent-reference-contrast intervals were below zero and all 40 contamination contrasts were adverse. A further pre-outcome-frozen transfer test applied the same design logic to SEP 1.4.1 photometry outside the A--E measurement implementation: across 100 matched identities and 900 measurements, all six contamination-versus-clean contrasts were interval-supported as adverse and all four contaminated-domain reference-contrast comparisons were interval-supported as improving with higher tested contrast. Workflow-relative behavior and vulnerability boundaries differed between the two controlled task families. These results show the value of preserving matched, cell-resolved validation structure and demonstrate implementation transfer without implying a task-independent workflow ordering. The study remains single-fidelity and synthetic; the reported boundaries are restricted to the tested domains and do not constitute observational validation.
\end{abstract}
