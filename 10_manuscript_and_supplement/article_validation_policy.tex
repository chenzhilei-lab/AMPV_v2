\begin{abstract}
AMPV is a reproducible validation methodology for mapping the reliability of astronomical photometric workflows under controlled compound contamination. The primary comet-like experiment evaluates workflows A--E on five reference-contrast levels and three contamination levels, using 300 matched stochastic identities to form 4,500 condition-specific scenes and 22,500 workflow evaluations. A pre-specified extension, internally frozen before outcome generation, evaluates a materially different compact-PSF task family with 300 identities and 18,000 evaluations of blind workflows A, B, C, and E. Performance is summarized with signed and absolute relative error and a predefined vulnerability indicator at $|e|>0.5$, with sample-identity bootstrap uncertainty and threshold sensitivity. In the comet-like family, all 60 adjacent-reference-contrast paired comparisons showed lower absolute error at the next tested contrast level, whereas contamination effects were not uniform across workflow--contrast cells. In contaminated compact-PSF cells, all 32 adjacent-reference-contrast comparisons showed the same improving direction, while all 40 tested contamination contrasts were adverse. Workflow-relative behavior and vulnerability boundaries differed between the two families; the compact-PSF advantage of Gaussian model fitting is interpreted as a task-specific model-match effect rather than universal superiority. The two controlled synthetic families therefore support AMPV as a method for recovering task-conditional, cell-resolved reliability structure rather than assigning a global workflow ranking. The study remains single-fidelity and synthetic, so the reported boundaries are restricted to the tested validation domains and do not constitute observational validation.
\end{abstract}
